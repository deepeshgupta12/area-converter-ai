What is the From Unit?

from_unit_section_html

A Hectare is a metric unit of area commonly used across India, defined as 10,000 square meters or a square measuring 100 meters on each side. It is widely recognized in national land records, municipal plans, and official property documents like RERA filings and loan applications. When a plot's area is provided in Hectares, it provides a standard and precise measurement for buyers and sellers nationwide.

In practical real estate contexts, Hectares appear frequently for large agricultural land, industrial plots, or layout plans in urban development. Government bodies, banks, and surveying agencies tend to standardize area in Hectares to maintain clarity across states. For example, in metro cities such as Mumbai, larger residential or commercial projects often have their total land shown in Hectares.

The concept of Hectare originates from the metric system developed in France during the 18th century and later adopted globally, including India. It replaced older, region-specific units to bring uniformity in land measurement. Being part of the metric system aligns Hectare with other units like meters and kilometers, making it universally understood in technical fields.

Besides real estate, Hectares are used extensively in agriculture, infrastructure planning, architecture, and land surveying. It assists professionals in calculating area accurately after measuring lengths and breadths in meters. The typical area calculation is obtained by multiplying the length and breadth measurements, which are converted into square meters and subsequently into Hectares for convenience.

What is the To Unit?

to_unit_section_html

Bigha is a traditional Indian unit of area measurement used widely across many states such as Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, and West Bengal. Unlike the Hectare, Bigha is not standardized nationwide and often varies locally, but it remains crucial for land dealings in rural and semi-urban India where people commonly reference land area in Bighas.

In Indian real estate, Bigha is preferred in land registration, broker negotiations, agricultural property sales, and registration papers in these regions. Many local land records and sale deeds mention Bigha, making it an essential unit for anyone converting land size from Hectare to understand regional land values properly.

The origin of Bigha dates back to pre-metric times when land was measured using customary units based on local practice. Though its size varies slightly by location, it generally corresponds to an area roughly between 1,500 and 2,700 square meters, much smaller than a Hectare, emphasizing the importance of accurate conversions.

Bigha is deeply embedded in domains like farming land transactions, surveying by local authorities, and village-level architecture or construction planning. Land professionals calculate area by measuring lengths and breadths (usually in feet or yards) and then converting these to Bigha through regional conversion factors. This traditional unit continues to play a significant role where metric units have not fully replaced customary measures.
