What is the From Unit?

from_unit_section_html

A Hectare is a metric unit of area measurement commonly used in India to measure large land parcels. One hectare represents an area of 10,000 square meters, roughly a square of 100 meters on each side. It's a simple and standard way to describe land size, especially for agricultural fields, large plots, and infrastructure projects.

In real estate and construction across India, hectares are often found in official land records, municipal documents, RERA filings, government land acquisition files, and loan application papers. Planners and engineers use hectares to estimate large-scale land areas with metric precision, making it common for regional and national authorities.

The hectare originated from the metric system, officially defined in Europe, and was adopted in India during metrication for standardizing land measurement. Unlike traditional Indian units, the hectare is globally recognized and replaces older local measurement units in many states. Its exact size simplifies calculations and planning.

Beyond real estate, hectares appear in agriculture for crop planning, in surveying for mapping land extents, in architecture and infrastructure projects, and during environmental assessments. When measuring area in hectares, the basic formula is Length × Breadth, where length and breadth are measured in meters, giving a final area in square meters that converts directly to hectares (1 hectare = 10,000 m²).

What is the To Unit?

to_unit_section_html

An Acre is an imperial unit of area measurement still widely used in Indian real estate markets alongside metric units. One acre equals about 4,046.86 square meters, or roughly 43,560 square feet, making it slightly smaller than half a hectare. Acres are familiar for land deals, especially outside large cities and in older property documents.

Across India, acres are prevalent in rural and semi-urban land transactions, often referenced in sale deeds, land records, and site plans. Many states and farmers still prefer quoting land in acres when selling or leasing agricultural land. Even in metro cities like Delhi or Bangalore, acreage appears during the evaluation of large plots before subdivision or development.

The acre has British imperial origins and entered Indian land measurement systems during colonial times. Despite India’s adoption of the metric system, acres remain culturally entrenched and practical for understanding intermediate land sizes. This blend of metric and imperial units coexists in many urban growth areas.

Acres are used extensively in real estate for plotting, broking, and investment analysis. Surveyors and architects calculate area in acres when planning residential layouts or farm boundaries. The formula to find area in acres is also based on Length × Breadth, where length and breadth may be initially measured in feet or meters and then converted appropriately, with 1 acre representing a fixed, traditional size.
